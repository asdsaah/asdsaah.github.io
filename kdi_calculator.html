Krafft–Douthwaite Index Calculator

Hypothesis: Every student in the degree program occupies a measurable position somewhere between Veit Krafft (MF 27') and James Douthwaite (MF 27'). Krafft is assigned 0 KDI and Douthwaite is assigned 100 KDI. Scores below 0 or above 100 identify a remarkable specimen of forestry brevado.
Why these measurements?
The KDI is like a stand level metric you would get from a forest inventory. Beverage preference represents compositional ecology; hair and beard length provide follicular mensuration; vocalization measures acoustic dominance; rain tolerance represents climatic adaptation; and pant pockets represent field-equipment stocking. Each trait is normalized against the two reference individuals before the five scores are combined.

What the component scores mean

  1. Beer–Cider Index: compares the log ratio of beers to ciders. Higher component scores indicate a more cider-dominant, Douthwaite-like beverage assemblage. A 0.5 pseudocount allows zero-beer observations without dividing by zero.
  2. Follicular Hypotenuse Index: treats vertical head hair and beard length as the perpendicular legs of a right triangle. Its hypotenuse is a deliberately excessive measurement of total follicular extent.
  3. Vocalization Dominance Index: converts decibels to relative acoustic intensity before normalization. Higher values therefore increase rapidly as vocalization approaches Douthwaite levels.
  4. Precipitation Tolerance Index: measures time before rain gear is added. “Never” is represented by the fixed 120-minute observation limit so that it remains numerically measurable.
  5. Pant Stocking Index: counts functional pockets. Because Krafft has more pockets, high pocket counts move this component toward the Krafft end of the axis.

Step 1: transform each measurement

R = ln[(beers + 0.5) / (ciders + 0.5)]
H = √(hair² + beard²)
L* = 10(dB − 70) / 10
RP = ln[1 + min(rain minutes, 120)]
P = number of functional pockets

Step 2: normalize against the buddies

For transformed value x, Krafft reference xK, and Douthwaite reference xD:
z = (x − xK) / (xD − xK)
This gives Krafft z = 0 and Douthwaite z = 1 for every component. A negative denominator is acceptable and automatically handles traits such as pocket count, where Krafft has the larger raw value.

Step 3: construct the composite

Let the five normalized component values be zi. Their equal-weight mean and profile variance are:
z̄ = (z1 + z2 + z3 + z4 + z5) / 5
V = Σ(zi − z̄)² / 5

Step 4: apply Ashton’s Constant

By ceremonial convention, Ashton’s Constant is:
𝒜 = π / e ≈ 1.155727
The final equation is:
KDI = 100 × [z̄ + (𝒜 / 10)(2z̄ − 1)(V / (1 + V))]
The second term is the Ashton Polarization Correction. It slightly pushes a heterogeneous collection of traits toward whichever reference already dominates the mean. It vanishes for both reference individuals, ensuring their final scores remain exactly 0 and 100.
